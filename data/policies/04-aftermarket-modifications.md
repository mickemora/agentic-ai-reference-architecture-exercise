# Aftermarket Modifications and Causal Assessment

> Synthetic training policy. It is not a statement of any real manufacturer’s modification policy.

## 1.0 Policy principle

The presence of an aftermarket part or modification does not by itself determine coverage. The evaluation must establish whether the modification caused, contributed to, concealed, or materially changed the reported failure.

## 2.0 Causally related modification

A repair is excluded when documented technical evidence shows that the modification directly caused or materially contributed to the failure. The evidence must identify the modification, the affected system, the observed failure mechanism, and the causal relationship.

Examples include an over-current accessory damaging a protected electrical circuit or an unauthorized calibration operating a component outside the fictional design range.

## 3.0 Unrelated modification

When a modification exists but available evidence does not establish a causal relationship, the claim must route to human review. The system must not infer causation from proximity, appearance, brand, cost, or the mere presence of modified equipment.

## 4.0 Required evidence

The claim file should include photographs, diagnostic results, wiring or installation observations when relevant, and a technician statement explaining causality. Unsupported statements such as “modified vehicle” are insufficient for an automated denial recommendation.

## 5.0 Restoration and subsequent evaluation

Removing a modification does not automatically establish coverage. The reviewer must evaluate whether the modification caused lasting damage and whether the restored configuration permits a reliable diagnosis.
